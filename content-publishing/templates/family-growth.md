# Family growth

## Format

### Writing
Use first-person narrative and concrete scenes, actions, pauses and dialogue before reflection. Ask for missing details rather than inventing gestures, chronology, direct quotes, diagnoses or results. Attribute another person's interpretation as a perspective, not a proven cause. Preserve nuance: one easier morning does not mean permanent resolution.

Connect events with the writer's own emotional energy, outlook and actions where supported. Avoid turning the story into a list of parenting tricks or imposing AI content or professional positioning. Keep connected, readable paragraphs.

### Cover
Use a warm handwritten notebook style with natural light, cream paper and generous space. The user's latest approved reference determines exact font, palette, composition and small symbols. Preserve those details unless explicitly changed. Without a reference, use a generic notebook treatment rather than inventing a personal visual identity.

Do not automatically add a column name, observation-diary label, AI logo/avatar, AI series number or technical styling.

### Body layout
Use `layout_template: family-growth`: white background, generous margins, centered blue subheadings, blue narrative (#307DCA), black personal reflection (#222222), 17px body, line-height about 2, 26px paragraph spacing. Set paragraph `role: reflection` for reflections. Avoid decorative cards, borders and panels. Preserve approved text.

### Ending
Return naturally to a real scene or change in the writer. Include an author signature only when supplied and approved. Do not append an AI slogan, fixed AI ending, family column name or AI numbering.

## Example

Fictional structure: a difficult departure, a parent's earlier response, a conversation, one small change the next morning, and an honest reflection. All dialogue and events must be provided by the user before drafting a factual article.
